# Sangeet Ghar — Design Direction

## Reference observations

The provided references suggest an atmosphere-first radio experience: one dominant place-based scene, an editorial wordmark, oversized vernacular script, quiet status details, and a compact player that feels embedded in the room rather than imposed as a media application. This project will retain only that restraint and emotional immediacy. It will not reuse any branding, compositions, assets, copy, scenes, or navigation from those sites.

## Three directions considered

## Chosen approach: Himalayan Letterpress

### Design Movement

**Contemporary vernacular editorialism**, inspired by 1990s Nepali cultural periodicals, letterpress ephemera, and the intimate object-design of a well-kept radio.

### Core Principles

1. **Atmosphere before interface.** The page should resemble a listening room, with controls treated as considered furniture rather than an application chrome.
2. **Printed matter, not digital gloss.** Paper grain, fine rules, ink-like contrast, and irregular floral lattice linework create warmth without imitating a vintage interface.
3. **Bilingual clarity.** Devanagari gives the identity cultural texture while English keeps the experience welcoming to the global Nepali community and curious new listeners.
4. **Slow confidence.** A small number of large, meaningful objects and generous negative space make listening feel unhurried.

### Color Philosophy

The base is a lightly imperfect **handmade-paper ivory** rather than pure white; it gives text and ornament the feel of printed ink. **Deep ink-navy** carries the editorial weight and delivers strong contrast. A single ownable **vermilion madder** accent—like a stamped radio-program seal—signals live playback, focus, and touch points. A muted **brass-gold** supports warmth in thin dividers and data marks, never as an ornamental overload.

### Layout Paradigm

The page is a **single vertical music folio**. A masthead sits above an asymmetrical listening stage: editorial copy holds the left column, while a large physical-record-like player composition anchors the right and partially crosses a vertical frame rule. On mobile, the stage becomes a deliberate sequence—identity, statement, player—rather than a shrunken desktop mosaic.

### Signature Elements

1. A **radio-seal disc** with concentric printed rings, responding quietly to audio state.
2. A **subtle Nepali window lattice** built from fine lines and repeated in borders, rather than using literal landmarks or clichés.
3. **Letterpress registration marks and caption rules** to create a publication-like rhythm around the listening state.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interaction should be immediate, generous, and legible. The main play action is the largest tactile object; track movement is relegated to simple framed controls. Status language uses personal, non-technical verbs such as “Tap to let the room play.” No control should reveal a library, a queue, or an algorithmic decision.

### Animation

The player disc may rotate almost imperceptibly only while music plays. Its status dot softly pulses at low amplitude, and the cover image receives a single 180ms fade on state changes. Button presses scale to 0.97. All non-essential animation is disabled for reduced-motion preferences; state changes remain fully visible without movement.

### Typography System

**Tiro Devanagari Hindi** gives the Nepali wordmark and supporting Devanagari copy a literary, archival character. **DM Serif Display** handles large English editorial statements, while **DM Mono** establishes utilitarian labels, live status, and metadata. Headings use a generous display scale with close-but-not-tight tracking; labels remain uppercase, small, and widely tracked for a print-program character.

### Brand Essence

**Sangeet Ghar is a quiet digital room for listeners everywhere to keep timeless Nepali music close.** Its personality is **warm, considered, and enduring**.

### Brand Voice

The voice is compact, observant, and welcoming without exaggeration. Headlines should name the feeling of listening; controls should speak like a considerate host. Example lines: “A room for songs that stay with you.” and “Tap once. Let the memories arrive.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The logo is a **square radio-window glyph**: a hand-stamped vermilion square with a cream archway cut through its center and three small sound-wave bars rising from the threshold. It is symbolic, text-free, and remains recognizable as a favicon. The wordmark combines a large Devanagari “संगीत” above a measured “SANGEET GHAR” label.

### Signature Brand Color

**Madder Seal — #A63B2B.**

## Style Decisions

The final page will use original composition and art direction, with no visual or structural copying from the supplied reference sites. The YouTube player stays visually minimized and is only controlled through the custom listening room interface.
